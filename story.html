I. The Architecture of Childhood
Use code with caution. True luxury is not manufactured; it is born from the obsession to create. For Malachi, the obsession began in a cramped Houston apartment, armed only with cardboard and boundless imagination. Where others saw waste, he saw skyscrapers, fortresses, and infinite worlds. This was the genesis of a structural mind. From mapping out virtual terrains in early digital spaces to molding literal empires out of mud, puddles, and wild flora, Malachi learned a fundamental truth early on: if a world does not suit you, you must build your own. II. The Artifacts of Evolution The transition from raw imagination to wearable art was inevitable. In the halls of high school, Malachi began engineering timepieces from foil, plastic, and paper clips—sculptures for the wrist that caught the eyes of peers and sparked his first taste of independence. Then came the needle and thread. A heavy sewing machine transformed his vision from rigid structures into fluid, tactile forms. He began translating raw human experience into physical objects.
